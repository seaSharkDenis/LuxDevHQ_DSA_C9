# Duka Smart Electronics — Business Insights

All money figures use **Completed orders only**, in Kenyan Shillings (KSh). Revenue is
`quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount / 100)` and profit is revenue minus `quantity * cost_price`, as the
business rules define them. "Today" means **30 Sep 2026**, the latest order date in the data.

**The business at a glance:** 800 orders from 150 customers. 634 orders were completed, bringing in
**KSh 24.16M revenue** and **KSh 2.89M profit**, an overall margin of **12.0%**. The average completed
order is worth **KSh 38,100** (Q13, Q14).

---

## 1. Phones and laptops bring in the revenue, but accessories make the profit

**Finding (Q33, Q42):** Mobile Phones (KSh 6.94M, 28.7% of revenue) and Computing (KSh 6.60M, 27.3%)
are the biggest categories, but their margins are thin at about **10.5%**. Accessories bring in only
**4.5% of revenue (KSh 1.08M)**, yet at a **42.4% margin** they earn KSh 456,746 in profit. That is the
same profit as Home Appliances (KSh 458,034), which needs almost four times the revenue to get there.
At product level (Q37), chargers and earbuds keep about **50%** of every shilling. Big-ticket items
keep very little: the Hotpoint Refrigerator keeps 4.0%, the Samsung 55in TV 4.6% and the iPhone 13 6.2%.

**Why it matters:** Every KSh 1,000 of accessories adds about KSh 424 to profit. The same KSh 1,000
spent on a TV or fridge adds about KSh 50. Growing the high-revenue categories alone will not grow
profit much.

**Recommendation:** Treat accessories as the profit engine. Bundle them with every phone, laptop
and TV sale (case + charger + earbuds), train staff to attach at least one accessory per device sale,
and display them at the till. For the low-margin big-ticket items, negotiate better supplier
prices rather than pushing more volume.

## 2. Discounts of 20% or more lose money on every sale

**Finding (Q33, discount breakdown):** Undiscounted orders earn a **17.5%** margin. The margin falls
as the discount rises: **6.7% at 10%**, **2.5% at 15%**, and below zero after that. Orders with
**20% off lose 2.2%**, and orders with **25% off lose 12.0%**. The 45 completed orders with 20–25%
discounts lost **KSh 68,700**. Across all discount levels, **62 completed orders were sold below cost**,
losing **KSh 141,979** in total.

**Why it matters:** Phones, laptops and TVs only have a 5–11% margin to begin with, so any discount
above about 10% on them gives away the whole profit and more. The business is paying customers to
take its stock.

**Recommendation:** Cap discounts at **10%** on Mobile Phones, Computing, Electronics and Home
Appliances. Anything above that should need a manager's approval and a stated reason, such as
clearing old stock. If promotions are needed, give a free accessory instead of a price cut, since an
accessory costs the shop much less than it is worth to the customer.

## 3. Sales are growing fast, but there is a large slump every January

**Finding (Q31, Q38–Q40, Q46):** Revenue for **January–September 2026 was KSh 12.83M**, against
KSh 6.89M for the same months of 2025, a rise of **86%**. The three best months are **August 2026
(KSh 2.24M)**, **September 2026 (KSh 2.09M)** and **June 2026 (KSh 2.04M)**. However, the
**December 2025** festive peak (KSh 2.03M) was followed by **January 2026, the lowest month in the
whole dataset at KSh 322,433**, a **fall of 84%** in one month (Q40). Revenue only climbed back above
KSh 1M in March.

**Why it matters:** The business is growing quickly, but January brings in about a sixth of
December's revenue while rent, salaries and stock costs stay the same. When school fees are due in
January, customers spend less on electronics.

**Recommendation:** Plan stock and cash for a quiet Q1. Order heavily for November–December and
lightly for January. Run a January "back-to-school" campaign on lower-priced items that students
need (Tecno and Infinix phones, earbuds, laptops on instalment), and offer M-Pesa or instalment
payment plans so customers can buy without paying the full price at once.

## 4. One customer in three has stopped buying, including six VIPs

**Finding (Q34):** Of the 132 customers with at least one completed order, **47 (36%) have not
completed an order in over 90 days**. Together they have spent **KSh 4.60M**, or **19% of all
revenue**. Among them are **6 VIP** and **7 Corporate** customers. The two most valuable lapsed
customers are **Janet Korir (VIP, KSh 593,882, last order 114 days ago)** and **Diana Kiprop
(Corporate, KSh 529,880, 98 days ago)**. In addition, **12 registered customers have never placed an
order at all** (Q23).

**Why it matters:** Keeping an existing customer costs far less than finding a new one. Most of
these customers have bought before, some of them many times, and the business is currently doing
nothing to bring them back.

**Recommendation:** Start a win-back programme. The sales team should call every lapsed VIP and
Corporate customer personally this month. Retail customers who have lapsed should receive an SMS
or M-Pesa voucher (for example, a free accessory with their next purchase). Run Q34 every month as
an early-warning report, and send the 12 customers who never ordered a welcome offer.

## 5. A fifth of customers bring in almost two-thirds of the revenue

**Finding (Q41, Q45):** Customers were placed into bands by their percentile rank on total spend.
The **top 20% (27 customers, each spending KSh 322K–848K)** generated **62.5% of revenue
(KSh 15.10M)**. The **bottom 50% (67 customers, under KSh 90K each)** generated only **11.0%**.
Percentiles were used rather than fixed KSh amounts because spending is very uneven, from KSh 1,899
to KSh 848,119, so a fixed cut-off would put almost everyone in one band. The **top five customers
are all in the "Retail" segment** (Q46): Brian Chebet (KSh 848,119), Peter Ndungu, Lucy Njoroge,
Nancy Korir and Purity Ochieng. Each has spent KSh 699K–848K across 15–22 orders.

**Why it matters:** Losing even a few High Value customers would cause a noticeable drop in revenue.
The business's own "VIP" label is not identifying them: its best customers are tagged as Retail and
receive no special treatment.

**Recommendation:** Re-tag customers based on what they actually spend. Move the High Value band
into the VIP programme and review the list every quarter. Give these customers a named account
contact, early access to new phones and laptops, and free delivery. For Medium Value customers, the
goal is to move them up a band, for example by offering a reward for reaching the next spending level.

## 6. Nairobi is 40% of the business, and Machakos punches above its weight

**Finding (Q17, Q25):** Nairobi produces **KSh 9.58M (40% of revenue)** from 41 buying customers.
Mombasa is second at KSh 3.16M. **Machakos has only 3 customers but the highest spend per customer of
any town (KSh 248,367)**. Thika (KSh 116,358 per customer) and Meru (KSh 136,733) have the lowest.

**Why it matters:** Relying so heavily on one city is a risk. A new competitor or a disruption in
Nairobi would hit 40% of revenue at once. Machakos shows that customers in smaller towns will spend
heavily once they are reached.

**Recommendation:** Keep Nairobi strong, but make growth outside it a target. Machakos is the best
place to test this, through local marketing and partnerships with businesses there. In Thika and
Meru, where spend per customer is low, push accessory bundles and phone upgrades to existing
customers.

## 7. Cancelled and pending orders hold back KSh 6.6M, mostly paid by M-Pesa

**Finding (Q20):** **11.25% of all orders (90) were cancelled**, worth **KSh 3.47M** at order value.
Another **76 orders (KSh 3.18M) are still pending**. Together, that is more than a quarter of what was
actually collected. M-Pesa orders are cancelled most often (**12.5%**), followed by Cash (12.1%). Bank
Transfer orders are cancelled least (**4.8%**). Accessories and Mobile Phones have the highest
cancellation rates (about 13% each).

**Why it matters:** Each cancellation wastes staff time and ties up stock that could have been sold
to someone else. The pending orders are revenue that is nearly won but not yet collected.

**Recommendation:** Chase pending orders now. Phone each customer and send them an M-Pesa payment
prompt. To reduce cancellations, take an M-Pesa deposit when an order is placed, and only hold
stock for a limited time. Track the reason for each cancellation (out of stock, price, changed mind)
so the main causes can be fixed.
